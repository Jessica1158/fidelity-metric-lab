Entity Inconsistency Injection

Entity Inconsistency was introduced by altering one later mention of the main entity in selected campaigns while preserving the canonical entity_id. Only the surname was changed, limiting the textual modification while creating a controlled identity inconsistency. A single fixed injection plan determined both the target message and replacement name for every campaign. Severity was defined by the prevalence of affected campaigns at 10%, 30%, and 60%, using nested campaign subsets so that the lower-severity conditions are contained within the higher-severity conditions. All timestamps, channels, senders, recipients, message order, references, and ground-truth entity identifiers were preserved.

Element Choice
Model sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2
Output dimension 384
Campaign input One ordered serialized text per campaign
Message information included order, channel, generic sender→recipient role, text
Excluded campaign_id, message_id, entity_id, reference_id, parent_message_id, defect labels
Encoding Same pretrained model for all conditions
Normalization L2, normalize_embeddings=True
Fine-tuning None
StandardScaler No
Truncation Not allowed silently; validate that each campaign fits
Batch size 64

all-MiniLM-L6-v2 is a general Sentence Transformers model producing 384-dimensional dense embeddings. Its default maximum input is 256 word pieces, which is why we will explicitly verify that none of our serialized campaigns is truncated.

Sequence-Length Check. The selected embedding model supports a maximum sequence length of 256 tokens. The serialized campaigns had a mean length of approximately 200 tokens and a maximum of 261 tokens. Only 2.67% of the evaluated campaign-condition instances exceeded the model limit, but silent truncation was avoided because even a small truncated segment could contain the injected inconsistency. Therefore, campaigns exceeding the limit will be split at message boundaries and combined into a single campaign-level embedding using the fixed chunking procedure.

chunk_validation_records = []

for condition in [
    "clean",
    0.10,
    0.30,
    0.60
]:

    serialized_df = (
        entity_serialized[
            condition
        ]
    )

    for _, row in serialized_df.iterrows():

        chunks = split_campaign_into_chunks(
            row["serialized_text"],
            embedding_model
        )

        chunk_lengths = []

        for chunk in chunks:

            length = len(
                embedding_model.tokenizer(
                    chunk,
                    add_special_tokens=True,
                    truncation=False
                )["input_ids"]
            )

            chunk_lengths.append(
                length
            )

            assert (
                length
                <= embedding_model.max_seq_length
            )

        chunk_validation_records.append({
            "condition": condition,
            "campaign_id":
                row["campaign_id"],
            "n_chunks":
                len(chunks),
            "max_chunk_tokens":
                max(chunk_lengths)

Semantic Representation Validation¶

The Entity Inconsistency experiment preserved the same 300 evaluation campaigns across all conditions. Defect prevalence was controlled using nested subsets containing 30, 90, and 180 affected campaigns for the 10%, 30%, and 60% severity levels, respectively. Only message text was modified; timestamps, channels, message order, participant identifiers, references, and canonical entity identifiers remained unchanged.

Each campaign was serialized as an ordered sequence containing message order, communication channel, generic sender/recipient roles, and message content. The serialization produced exactly 300 campaign texts per condition, with exactly 30, 90, and 180 serialized campaigns differing from the clean baseline.

The frozen semantic encoder was sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (revision 1110a243fdf4706b3f48f1d95db1a4f5529b4d41), producing 384-dimensional L2-normalized embeddings. The model supports a maximum sequence length of 256 tokens. Mean campaign length was approximately 200 tokens and the maximum was 261 tokens. Eight campaigns per condition exceeded the model limit.

To prevent silent truncation from removing potentially defective content, long campaigns were split at message boundaries. All resulting chunks contained at most 256 tokens, only two chunks were required for any single campaign, and each condition produced 308 chunks for 300 campaigns. Chunk embeddings were aggregated using a token-weighted mean and subsequently L2-normalized to obtain one 384-dimensional vector per campaign.

Representation Response to Entity Inconsistency¶

The general semantic representation responded to the injected entity changes without using any entity-specific consistency indicator. Among affected campaigns, the mean Euclidean change between the clean and defective normalized embeddings remained relatively stable across severity levels: 0.0634 at 10%, 0.0597 at 30%, and 0.0585 at 60%.

In contrast, the mean embedding change across all campaigns increased from 0.0063 to 0.0179 and 0.0351 as defect prevalence increased from 10% to 30% and 60%. This increase is approximately proportional to the fraction of affected campaigns. This behavior is consistent with the experimental design: severity changes the prevalence of the defect, while the per-campaign intervention remains fixed.

Importantly, the relatively small change observed in the 384-dimensional normalized embeddings indicates that the semantic representation does not act as an explicit Entity Inconsistency detector. Instead, the name substitution produces a subtle change within a general-purpose semantic representation. The subsequent paired C2ST, MMD, and Energy Distance experiments will test whether these representation-level changes are sufficient to distinguish the defective distributions at different prevalence levels.

# helper to save results 
def extract_metric_result(
    result,
    metric_name
):
    """
    Normalize output from the three metric functions.
    """

    if "p" in result:
        p_value = result["p"]

    elif "p_value" in result:
        p_value = result["p_value"]

    else:
        raise KeyError(
            "No p-value found in metric result."
        )

    if metric_name == "C2ST":

        statistic = result.get(
            "accuracy",
            result.get(
                "statistic",
                np.nan
            )
        )

    elif metric_name == "MMD":

        statistic = result.get(
            "statistic",
            result.get(
                "mmd2",
                result.get(
                    "mmd",
                    np.nan
                )
            )
        )

    elif metric_name == "Energy":

        statistic = result.get(
            "statistic",
            result.get(
                "energy",
                result.get(
                    "energy_distance",
                    np.nan
                )
            )
        )

    else:
        raise ValueError(
            "Unknown metric."
        )

    reject = result.get(
        "reject",
        p_value < ALPHA
    )

    return (
        float(statistic),
        float(p_value),
        bool(reject)
    )

Cómo interpretar la curva

A 10% de campañas afectadas, MMD tiene power 0.640 y Energy 0.605. Es decir, aun cuando en cada muestra de 100 campañas entran en promedio solamente unas 10 campañas defectuosas, ambas métricas detectan el cambio alrededor del 60–64% de las veces.

Al llegar al 30%, ambas alcanzan:

$$ \text{power}=1.00 $$

y se mantienen en 1.00 al 60%. Esto significa que, con esta representación semántica, a partir de aproximadamente 30 campañas afectadas dentro de una muestra de 100, el cambio distribucional resulta suficientemente fuerte para ser detectado prácticamente siempre por MMD y Energy bajo esta configuración.

La representación contiene información suficiente sobre Entity Inconsistency, pero el C2ST basado en regresión logística lineal tiene dificultades para convertir esa alteración semántica sutil en una frontera de clasificación.

Esto se parece conceptualmente a lo que ocurrió con el variance shift de Week 3: no basta con preguntar si “el defecto existe”; el resultado depende de la combinación

defect+representation+metric/test architecture.la representación no es el cuello de botella principal

En nuestras implementaciones estamos usando estimadores tipo U-statistic. Estos estimadores pueden producir valores ligeramente negativos en muestras finitas aunque las cantidades poblacionales correspondientes sean no negativas.

Entity Inconsistency — Paired Sensitivity Results¶

Entity Inconsistency produced markedly different detection behavior across the three metrics despite all tests receiving exactly the same 384-dimensional campaign embeddings.

MMD and Energy Distance were substantially more sensitive to the injected defect. At 10% defect prevalence, empirical power was 0.640 for MMD and 0.605 for Energy Distance. When prevalence increased to 30%, both metrics reached a power of 1.000 and remained fully saturated at the 60% severity level.

C2ST showed a substantially different response. Its empirical rejection rates were 0.000, 0.025, and 0.165 at 10%, 30%, and 60% defect prevalence, respectively. Mean classification accuracy increased only from 0.503 at 10% to 0.527 at 60%, indicating that the linear logistic-regression classifier had difficulty separating clean and defective campaign embeddings.

This contrast is important because all three methods operated on the same semantic representation. Consequently, the weak C2ST result cannot be interpreted simply as a failure of the embedding representation to retain the Entity Inconsistency signal. MMD and Energy Distance demonstrate that a detectable distributional change is present in the representation. Instead, the result suggests that the particular classifier used by C2ST is relatively insensitive to the form of distributional change produced by this defect.

The clean-vs-clean null experiment showed Type I error rates of 0.035 for C2ST and 0.055 for both MMD and Energy Distance, all close to the nominal alpha level of 0.05. The high power observed for MMD and Energy Distance therefore does not appear to result from uncontrolled false-positive behavior.

Across the 200 repetitions, samples contained an average of 10.02, 29.90, and 59.80 affected campaigns at the nominal 10%, 30%, and 60% severity levels. This confirms that the prevalence-based severity design behaved as intended while retaining natural sampling variability.

Overall, Entity Inconsistency reveals a clear metric-specific sensitivity difference: the general semantic representation preserves sufficient information for kernel- and distance-based two-sample tests to detect the defect, while the linear C2ST remains largely insensitive except at the highest prevalence level.
